## Router

Routing is hand-rolled on the History API in `src/router/` and is the one piece of infrastructure written by hand:
`history.ts` (external store for `useSyncExternalStore`, `navigate(to, { replace })`, a key per history entry in `history.state` with scroll positions kept in `sessionStorage` so Back, Forward and reload restore them, `document.startViewTransition` with `flushSync` unless `prefers-reduced-motion`),
`match.ts` (route table with `:param` segments, not-found fallback),
`router.tsx` (provider, `useRoute()`, outlet, title, focus to the page `h1` and scroll after navigation),
`link.tsx` (intercepts plain left clicks only),
`announcer.tsx` (polite `role="status"` region that reads the new page title).
Focus, scroll, announcement and the view transition happen only when the pathname changes; a search-only change updates the location and nothing else, except that a popstate to another entry restores that entry's remembered scroll. A restored position the document cannot hold yet (the ledger's result lands after the popstate) is held for up to 1 s: a `ResizeObserver` on `document.body` re-applies it once when the document grows tall enough, and a wheel, touch or key event or the next navigation drops the hold. A popstate that leaves the URL unchanged (a fragment entry) and a replace navigation both move the location's `key` to the entry's key, so scroll is never filed under a stale key. A failed view transition is reported once: `updateCallbackDone` rejections go to `reportError`, while `ready` (a skipped transition) and `finished` (the same error again) are swallowed.
The initial load neither moves focus nor announces; fragment-only navigations (the skip link) are ignored by the router.
History entry keys are a per-session counter persisted under `daybook:history-seq` in `sessionStorage` (in memory if storage is unavailable).
Scroll is only remembered while the displayed page matches the committed location, so a pending view transition cannot misattribute a position.
Same-page hash links are left to the browser; a link to a fragment on another page scrolls that element into view after the commit.

## Domain

The window is 36 calendar months, October 2023 through September 2026, ending at the fixed today `2026-09-16T12:00:00Z` (`TODAY` in `src/domain/world.ts`); nothing is dated after today.
All dates are UTC, and `formatDate` formats in UTC so every visitor sees the same day.
`transaction(i)` seeds its own stream with `hashCombine(0xDA7B00, i)` and reads only constant world arrays, so it is pure, O(1) in `i`, and the worker can regenerate any row on its own.

Index scheme: indices `0 .. RECURRING_COUNT - 1` (766) are the recurring schedule, laid out month-major with 21 slots per month (rent on the 1st, 12 salaries on the 25th, 8 card-paid subscriptions on fixed days) for the 35 complete months, then only the 7 slots of September 2026 dated on or before today, then 24 quarterly tax payments (two alternating streams, paid on the 10th of January, April, July and October).
A recurring date after today is dropped rather than shown as pending, so September 2026 has rent and six subscriptions but no salaries; recurring rows are always booked, in the account currency.
Indices `766 .. 99_999` are organic: date first, then status, account, category (from per-account weights), counterparty, amount, currency (85 % the account's own) and, for expense categories, an optional active card of the same account.
Organic dates pick a month through a CDF whose per-day weight grows linearly from 1 to 2.5 across the window, then a uniform instant inside it (the final month is cut at today).
A row dated inside the last 14 days is pending with probability 0.6; 1 % of rows are declined, only on expense categories.

The Main account carries most revenue and all payroll and ends richest; Operations roughly breaks even; UK sales is modestly positive.
Running balance = `openingBalance` + the sum of non-declined amounts converted to the account currency (`accountAmount`); booked and pending both count.
Opening balances are 40,000.00 EUR, 20,000.00 USD and 15,000.00 GBP (lowest running balances without them: -6,736.20, -622.50 and -69.01); closing balances are 2,280,773.30 EUR, 69,132.47 USD and 214,850.84 GBP.
A test scans all 100,000 rows chronologically and asserts no account goes below zero.
Budgets are monthly amounts in EUR minor units, set near the last 12 complete months' average spend so some categories run over.
fx converts with integer rates per 100 EUR (EUR 100, USD 108, GBP 86) in one division, rounding half away from zero to integer minor units; a round trip is off by at most one minor unit.
VAT is 0 % for revenue, interest, salaries and tax, 7 % for meals, 19 % otherwise.

`monthlySeriesByAccount()` returns per account the income, expense (positive minor units) and net of the 35 complete months (October 2023 through August 2026) and the partial September 2026 separately as month to date, so the forecast never reads a month without payroll.
Forecast: income and expense are projected separately, each future month being the mean of the last three complete months times a seasonal factor (the mean of the same calendar month in prior years divided by the overall mean, clamped to 0.5..1.5, and 1 unless both means are positive), rounded half away from zero to integer minor units, with net = income - expense.
No seasonal factor hits the clamp for September to November 2026; forecast nets against mean monthly nets are 53,373.92, 13,677.03 and 143,327.95 EUR against 63,159.78 for Main, 39,609.89, -24,807.46 and 44,267.54 USD against 1,532.60 for Operations, and 33,120.12, -9,567.44 and 12,833.87 GBP against 5,298.78 for UK sales.

## Component library

`src/ui/<Name>/` holds one component per folder with its CSS Module and test; class names are joined with `cx()` from `src/ui/classes.ts`, which tolerates the `string | undefined` that CSS Modules return under `noUncheckedIndexedAccess`.
Focus has one rule in `base.css`: `:focus-visible` draws the 2 px `--color-focus` ring at 2 px offset, `[data-focus-ring]` draws it on the element right after a focused native input (the drawn checkbox box, switch track and theme segment, whose real inputs sit transparent on top of them), and `[data-focus-within-ring]` moves it from its `[data-ring-source]` input to the wrapper (the text field well) while other controls inside keep their own ring.
Dynamic values never use a `style` prop; they go through data attributes or custom properties written on the element through a ref.
Button reserves its width without JS: label and spinner share one grid cell, and while loading the label, rendered once, stays in flow and in the accessibility tree with `color: transparent` while the spinner takes the variant's `--button-foreground`.
Fields share `useFieldIds()`, `Field` and `FieldError` from `src/ui/field.tsx`: a visible label, an optional description and error joined into `aria-describedby`, `aria-invalid` while an error shows, and a polite live region that is always rendered so errors that appear on change are announced.
NumberField is a TextField over non-negative integer minor units (at most 1e15); while focused it keeps the raw draft, validates it on every change against the locale's decimal separator and grouping (pasted currency symbols and bidi marks are stripped) and reports `null` while the draft is invalid, shows the "Enter an amount like ..." error only once a blur leaves the draft invalid and until the next valid change ("Amount is too large." shows at once), and formats with the currency on blur only when the draft is valid.
The theme lives in `src/state/theme.ts`, a `useSyncExternalStore` store persisted under localStorage `theme` (`system`, `light`, `dark`) that writes the resolved scheme to `data-theme` on `<html>`, listens to `prefers-color-scheme` only in system mode, and follows `storage` events from other tabs.
Stage 1d adds a resolved-scheme snapshot and hook to the theme store so `/design` can re-measure contrast live when the scheme changes.
A one-statement inline script in `index.html` applies a stored `light` or `dark` before the stylesheet loads, allowed by its sha256 hash in the CSP; `main.tsx` then calls `initTheme()` before `createRoot`.
Component tests live next to the components but type-check in `tsconfig.node.json` with the other tests, so the jest-dom matcher types from `tests/unit/setup.ts` stay out of the app program.
`tests/unit/tokens-contrast.test.ts` parses the `light-dark()` colour tokens and guards the AA pairs in both themes; `/design` remains the visitor-facing measurement.
Menu and Tooltip use the Popover API and CSS anchor positioning with no positioning code: `src/ui/anchor.ts` derives a per-instance dashed ident from `useId()` and writes `anchor-name` on the trigger and `position-anchor` on the panel through ref callbacks on `element.style`, and the CSS Module places the panel with `position-area` and flips it with `position-try-fallbacks`.
Browser floor: Chromium 129+, Safari 26+ and Firefox 147+ (its anchor-positioning release). Chromium shipped the Popover API earlier and anchor positioning in 125, but the property names used here, `position-area` and `position-try-fallbacks`, arrived in 129 and 128 (125 had `inset-area` and `position-try-options`). The audience uses current browsers, there is no production polyfill, and Playwright runs Chromium.
Menu is a `popover="auto"` panel opened by a `popovertarget` button, so Escape and light dismiss come from the platform; ArrowDown or ArrowUp on the trigger opens it on the first or last item. The `toggle` event sets `aria-expanded`, focuses the first enabled item on open, and on close returns focus to the trigger when focus was inside the menu or fell to `<body>`: an outside click on a focusable element keeps that element focused, an outside click on anything else returns focus to the trigger. Items rove `tabIndex`, disabled items carry `aria-disabled` and are skipped by arrows and type-ahead, Tab closes the menu, the closed panel fades to `opacity: 0` and an item ignores activation unless the panel is still `:popover-open`.
Tooltip is a `popover="manual"` panel shown on pointer enter and focus and hidden on blur, on Escape from anywhere in the document (claimed with `preventDefault()` in the capture phase so an enclosing dialog or popover stays open), and 150 ms after the pointer leaves the trigger unless it enters the tooltip, whose own pointer leave hides it; that grace period does not change under reduced motion. The 300 ms open delay is a CSS `transition-delay` on opacity from `@starting-style`, carried by `--delay-tooltip`, which reduced motion zeroes, so the description is in the accessibility tree at once. The trigger comes from a render prop: the trigger element, a single focusable and never disabled element, spreads the tooltip props (`ref`, `aria-describedby` and the handlers), and any other description ids go through the Tooltip's `describedBy` prop, which is merged with the tooltip id into one `aria-describedby` token list. The ref hides the tooltip if the trigger leaves the DOM while it is open, and leaving a focused trigger with the pointer does not schedule the hide. `anchor-name` is a list too: `src/ui/anchor.ts` appends its name and removes only its own, so one element can anchor a menu and a tooltip. Its `--space-2` margin keeps it 8 px from the trigger and from every viewport edge when `position-area` clamps it near an edge.
jsdom has neither the Popover API nor `HTMLDialogElement.showModal()`. `tests/unit/popover.ts` installs a test-only polyfill: `showPopover`/`hidePopover`/`togglePopover`, a synchronous `toggle` event with `newState`/`oldState`, `popovertarget` invoker clicks, light dismiss and Escape for auto popovers, `:popover-open` mapped to a `data-popover-open` attribute in `matches()`, and a stylesheet that hides closed popovers and outranks jsdom's user-agent rule for open ones. `tests/unit/dialog.ts` adds `showModal`/`show`/`close` with the `close` event and Escape through a cancelable `cancel` event. Neither restores focus, so the components' own focus return is what the tests see, and both ignore an Escape whose `keydown` was already default-prevented, as the platform does.
Dialog is controlled: the native `<dialog>` follows its `open` prop in an effect, `showModal()` on open and `close()` in the cleanup, with focus returned to the element focused before opening, and centred with `margin: auto` because the `* { margin: 0 }` reset removes the user-agent centring. Escape fires a cancelable `cancel`, which the dialog prevents and reports through `onClose`, so only the prop closes it; a non-cancelable `cancel` is left alone; when Chrome's close-watcher anti-abuse skips `cancel` and the dialog closes anyway while `open` is still true, the `close` event calls `onClose` and the effect re-runs, re-opening it. Backdrop clicks are ignored.
Tabs activate automatically with Left, Right, Home and End, computed from the focused tab so a controlled parent that rejects a change does not strand focus; a panel has `tabIndex=0` only while it has no focusable descendant, re-checked by a `MutationObserver` on the selected panel, rebuilt only when the selection changes, when its content changes without a Tabs render. Menu, Tabs and Table share `src/ui/roving.ts`: `rovingIndex()` (first, last, next, previous, with wrap, step and skip-disabled) and `useElementMap()` for the per-key element refs. A disabled tab is a native disabled button, skipped by the arrows and never selected; the selected tab is marked by a `--rule-width-strong` accent underline through `text-decoration`, not a border, so forced colours do not paint the unselected tabs' transparent borders and nothing shifts.
Toasts live in `src/state/toast.ts`, a frozen-snapshot external store; `<ToastRegion>` is mounted once in the shell as a polite `role="status"` region and shows at most three toasts, the rest queue. Timers never read the clock: one 100 ms `setInterval` runs while toasts are visible and not paused and subtracts a tick from each visible toast's remaining time, so pausing simply stops the interval and resuming continues from what is left (within one tick). The region sets `aria-atomic="false"` so only the new toast is announced, each dismiss button is described by its toast title, and a dismiss or action from a focused button moves focus to the first control of the toast that takes its place, if any. The region pauses while the pointer is over it or focus is inside it, and re-checks focus whenever the list changes, because a dismissed toast takes focus with it without a `focusout`.
Toasts are raised after a dialog closes, never from inside an open modal dialog: `showModal()` makes everything outside the dialog inert, the fixed toast region included.
Stage 1c tokens: `--color-badge-neutral|positive|negative|warning|accent` are explicit badge surfaces and `--color-warning-text` (light `#6E4C00`, dark = warning) is the warning badge text, replacing 12 % `color-mix()` tints that measured 3.59 to 4.02 for warning in light; `tokens-contrast.test.ts` guards each text-on-badge pair at 4.5:1 in both themes. Also new: `--color-backdrop`, `--avatar-1..8`, `--duration-shimmer`, `--delay-tooltip` and layout sizes for popovers, tooltip, dialog, toast, table and avatar. `--z-popover` and `--z-dialog` are removed because Menu, Tooltip and Dialog render in the top layer and do not use `z-index`; `--z-sticky` and `--z-toast` stay.
Table keeps roving focus by row key, not index or object identity: the active row has `tabIndex=0`, Up, Down, Home, End and PageUp/PageDown (10 rows) move it, and the key survives re-renders with fresh row objects or reordering; arrows act only when the row itself has focus, Space toggles selection on a focused row, and row checkboxes are out of the tab order so one Tab stop reaches the rows. Sortable headers are buttons inside `<th>`, `aria-sort` sits only on the sorted column, and clicking proposes ascending first, then flips. `virtualised` switches to `role="grid"` with `aria-rowcount` (data rows plus the header row) and `aria-rowindex` from `rowIndexOffset`; the windowing itself is Stage 2. Under forced colours the selected row loses its background and inset shadow, so it gets a real `border-inline-start` in `Highlight`.

## Design page

`/design` measures instead of asserting. A hidden absolutely positioned probe span gets `var(--token)` written into one property at a time through `element.style.setProperty` (background-color for colours, font-size for type, inline-size for space, border-radius, box-shadow, transition-duration and transition-delay, transition-timing-function, z-index) and `getComputedStyle(probe)` reads the used value back, so `light-dark()`, `clamp()` and reduced-motion overrides are settled by the browser; reading the custom property on `:root` only returns the declared text, which the page shows next to the resolved value.
Contrast uses `src/ui/contrast.ts`, shared with `tests/unit/tokens-contrast.test.ts`: WCAG 2.1 relative luminance from hex, `rgb()`/`rgba()` (legacy and space syntax) and `color(srgb ...)`, a translucent foreground composited over an opaque background, and `null` for anything else (a translucent background, `oklch()`, system colours), which the page shows as "unmeasured". Ratios are cut to two decimals, never rounded up, and each pair carries `data-ratio` and `data-minimum` for the e2e check; 30 pairs: text and accent roles on the surfaces, muted text on the sunken table header, text on accent and negative fills including hover, badge text on badge surfaces, border-strong on the three surfaces at 3:1 non-text, and text on the eight avatar hues.
The measurement lives in React state and is keyed by the resolved scheme from `useResolvedScheme()` in `src/state/theme.ts` (a `useSyncExternalStore` snapshot of `data-theme`, falling back to the dark-scheme media query before `initTheme()`), the viewport width and the reduced-motion query; the probe carries that key as its React key, so a change remounts it and its ref callback measures again during commit, before paint. The page states which scheme it measured.
Focus visible is shown without focusing: the shared rule in `base.css` also matches `[data-force-focus]`, including the `[data-focus-ring]` sibling and `[data-focus-within-ring]` wrapper forms.
`scrollbar-gutter: stable` on `<html>` in `base.css` stays as the layout-shift guard for a page that gains a scrollbar after it first paints, as `/design` does when its lazy chunk renders. The route keeps a small statically imported shell (`src/pages/design.tsx`: the h1, the intro and a Skeleton fallback) and lazy-loads the rest from `src/pages/design/content.tsx` with `React.lazy`, so the router's focus-to-h1 and the announcer work before the chunk arrives and the chunk stays out of the initial JS that `verify-bundle` counts. A fragment such as `/design#motion` is scrolled to once the chunk has rendered.
The measurement key also carries `prefers-contrast: more` and `forced-colors: active`, and resize notifications are throttled to one per animation frame. Wide tables sit in an `overflow-x: auto` wrapper so the page never scrolls sideways at 375 px; a ResizeObserver makes the wrapper a focusable, labelled region only while it actually overflows. Focus visible on parts a component does not let through props (the Menu trigger, a Tab, a Table row) is set by a gallery-only ref callback that writes `data-force-focus` on the rendered element. Each keyboard sentence on the page restates a test title or assertion in the component's test file; behaviour that only the platform provides (the native select list, date input typing) is attributed to the browser.

## Ledger

The ledger worker owns filtering, sorting, search and totals and answers with ids only: `ids` is an exactly sized `Int32Array` whose buffer is transferred, at most 400,000 bytes for all 100,000 rows. The main thread renders each visible row from `transaction(id)`, which is pure and O(1), so no row data is cloned across the boundary, no page-window protocol exists, and a result is one allocation and one transfer however the user scrolls.
`src/worker/index.ts` holds the pure index and query functions and is tested in Node; `ledger.worker.ts` is only the message loop, `protocol.ts` the request and response unions (exhaustive switches end in `assertNever`), and `client.ts` the main-thread singleton.
Index layout, built once from `transaction(i)` for every i: `bookedAt` `Float64Array`; `amount` (own currency), `amountInEur` and `amountInAccount` `Int32Array`; `account`, `card` (0 = no card), `counterparty`, `currency`, `status` and `category` `Uint8Array` codes into the world arrays; `originalCategory`, a copy that the edits overlay resets from; one lowercased `counterpartyName + ' ' + reference` string per row; and one reusable `Int32Array` scratch buffer that a query fills before copying out the exact-sized result.
Sort orders are built lazily per column on first use and cached: a pre-sorted id permutation over a numeric key (booked time; the amount divided by its currency's rate per 100 EUR, so amounts in different currencies order exactly; counterparty name and category label ranks by `localeCompare(..., 'en')`; status in the order booked, pending, declined) with id ascending as the tie-break. A descending query walks the permutation backwards run by run of equal keys, so ties stay id ascending in both directions. A query is one O(n) walk of the permutation: typed predicates first, the substring match on the haystack last, never a per-query sort.
Filters: account, categories, cards (a card filter excludes rows without a card), statuses, `from`/`to` as whole UTC days inclusive (a reversed pair is ordered first, as is `min`/`max`), and `min`/`max` compared with the magnitude of the amount in the display currency, the selected account's currency or else EUR. Magnitude rather than signed amount because the NumberField takes non-negative amounts and nearly every row is a debit, so "between 50 and 500" means the size of the movement either way; the URL accepts only non-negative integers for both. The worker lowercases `q`, splits it on runs of whitespace and requires every word to occur in the row's haystack, in any order, so `pay card` matches `Card payment`; the URL keeps `q` as typed so a trailing space survives the round trip while typing.
Totals: `count` covers every matching row; `debits` (positive), `credits` and `net = credits - debits` cover booked and pending rows only, each row converted with `convert` into the display currency before summing.
The `edits` message carries the full list of category edits; the worker resets the category column from `originalCategory`, applies the list and drops the cached category order, so clearing an edit restores the seeded category. `client.ts` forwards the category edits from `src/state/edits.ts` whenever that snapshot's `categories` object changes, posts them first to every worker it spawns, and posts them immediately to a live one.
The client spawns the worker on the first request, numbers requests, and keeps one pending map; every `query` carries a channel, the `useId()` of the calling `useLedgerQuery` instance, and a newer query resolves the older pending queries on the same channel only with `null` (superseded), whose late responses are ignored; other channels and `history` and `series` requests are never superseded. `terminate()` resolves pending queries with `null` and rejects pending `history`, `series` and the ready promise with a terminated error; in development `import.meta.hot.dispose` stops the edits forwarding and calls it. A worker `error` or `messageerror` rejects every pending request and the ready promise, terminates the worker, and the next request spawns a fresh one with the edits re-sent.
`ready` carries no build time: timing in the worker would need a `performance.now()` exemption from the determinism bans, so the build is measured in `tests/unit/ledger-perf.test.ts` instead.
`series` returns `monthlySeriesByAccount()` for one account in its currency, or the three accounts summed month by month in EUR (each account's monthly total converted, then added), computed once and cached in the worker.
`useLedgerQuery(filter)` (`src/state/query.ts`) serialises the filter, defers that string with `useDeferredValue`, posts the parsed deferred filter from an effect, and keeps the last landed result until a newer one lands; an effect cleanup drops any response for a value that is no longer current, on top of the client's superseding. The hook also reads the edits store's `categories` snapshot through `useSyncExternalStore` and re-posts the query when it changes, after the client has already posted the new edits, so category filters and totals follow a bulk categorise or a detail edit. A store commit freezes and replaces only the edited section and keeps the other sections' references, so a note, budget or card edit leaves `categories` untouched and posts no query; a `storage` event from another tab may rebuild every section. `isStale` is true while nothing has landed, the landed result is for another filter, or the deferred value lags the live one. A failed worker rethrows the error from the hook during render, for the nearest error boundary.
URL codec (`src/state/url.ts`): `parseFilter` reads `location.search` tolerantly and `serializeFilter` returns `''` or a `?`-prefixed canonical string, so `pathname + serializeFilter(filter)` is always a valid target. A reversed `from`/`to` or `min`/`max` pair passes through unchanged, so the URL mirrors what the user typed and the range fields show their own validation; the worker orders each pair before filtering, so `min=100&max=5` returns the same ids as `min=5&max=100`. Normalising drops a `q` that `encodeURIComponent` rejects (a lone surrogate). A Vitest property test round-trips 500 states drawn with `mulberry32(0x5eed)`, including invalid dates, negative and fractional amounts, duplicates and reserved characters in `q`.
The edits store persists `{ version: 1, categories, notes, budgets, cards }` under localStorage `edits`; invalid JSON, another version or a non-object section resets it to empty, an invalid single entry is dropped, and an edit set back to the seeded value is removed rather than stored.

## Ledger page

`/transactions` keeps only the `h1` in the route component and lazy-loads the rest (`src/pages/transactions/content.tsx`) like `/design`, so focus-to-h1 never races the chunk and the initial bundle carries neither the ledger, the Table, the form controls nor the worker client.
Filter state is the URL only: the page parses `location.search`, and every control writes back through `writeFilter` (`src/pages/transactions/filters.ts`), which builds `pathname + serializeFilter(next)` from the current router snapshot at event time, so no handler closes over the filter and the ledger's props stay referentially stable while the URL changes.
History: filters, sort, the account switcher and Clear filters push a new entry, so Back steps through filter states; search writes are debounced 150 ms in the header and replace the current entry, except the first write of a search session (`q` empty to non-empty) and clearing `q` entirely, which push (`writeSearch` in `filters.ts`; a whitespace-only `q` counts as empty, and the write is dropped when `window.location` is no longer on `/transactions`, so a timer firing during a pending view transition cannot pull the user back), so Back leaves a search in one step and typing does not flood history. A replace navigation neither bumps the history key nor remembers scroll, so it does not overwrite the entry's restored scroll position. A pending search write is cancelled when the history key changes (another filter, Back or Forward reset the draft to the URL's `q`), and Enter on `/transactions` flushes it at once. A popstate that keeps the pathname restores the scroll remembered for the entry it lands on whenever the history key changed.
Result landing: module-level `startTransition` around the landed result in `useLedgerQuery`; `isStale` is derived from deferred-vs-live search and a landed-key comparison (landed search and category edits against the current ones).
`aria-busy` on the results region follows that URL-driven query only; the 150 ms draft window in the header is not announced, because flipping it at keystroke time would be a commit outside the search field.
The header carries the account switcher (writes `account` into the current query on any page, dropping cards of other accounts on the ledger) and the search field (writes `q` on `/transactions`, submits to `/transactions?q=` elsewhere). The search state lives in the header, outside the ledger subtree.
Zero wasted re-renders: the ledger receives only the landed ids, two sort primitives, the balance map and the selection, all stable across a keystroke; `src/pages/transactions.test.tsx` wraps the sidebar, the totals and the ledger in three `<Profiler>`s and counts row renders, and asserts zero commits and zero row renders from a search keystroke until the deferred query is posted and answered, then exactly one ledger commit, and one ledger commit for a sidebar checkbox. Passing `filter.sort.column` straight into the JSX made the compiler key the element on the whole `filter` object and cost one wasted commit per keystroke; destructuring the primitives first fixed it.
The last wasted commit (measured with logging patched into `react-dom-client.development.js`) came from the Table's mount layout effect calling `setView` with an unchanged window on SyncLane: React's eager bailout skipped the render but left the update in the hook queue. The next Table render was the lane-512 transition that lands the result, which skipped the SyncLane update and left lane 2 on the fiber, so React flushed it right after the commit as a `nested-update` commit with identical state. Comparing each measured window against the last requested one, kept in a ref, and calling `setView` only on a real change removed it.
Balance: shown only with one account and the booked-at sort; a running sum of `accountAmount` from the account's opening balance over the result in chronological order, computed once per landed result on the main thread and kept in page state alongside the ids it belongs to, keyed by transaction id. With further filters active it is the running sum of the filtered rows.

Virtualisation (`src/ui/Table/Table.tsx`, `virtual.ts`): the page is the scroll container (window scrolling), and nothing nests a vertical scroller; in the virtual Table the `thead` itself is the sticky element (`.virtual > thead`, its `th` cells go static), so one box sticks and the scroll offset and the e2e helper read that box's height. `measureGeometry` (`virtual.ts`) takes the covered band as the `thead`'s bottom clamped to the viewport (`stickyHeight`), which covers a stuck, a natural and a pushed-up header in one expression, so the tab stop and the Page keys reserve no header band where the header is off-screen; inside the scroll region below 1280 px the rows' `scroll-margin-block-start` is 0 for the same reason. The Table slices the full `ArrayLike` itself: a scroll, resize or body `ResizeObserver` callback reads the `tbody` top and the row height (measured from a rendered row, 36 px from `--table-row-height`) and stores the first and last visible row indices in state, so it re-renders only when a row boundary is crossed; the render adds 4 rows of overscan on each side (`OVERSCAN_ROWS`). The space above and below the window is two `display: block` pseudo-elements on the `tbody` whose block sizes come from `--table-space-before` and `--table-space-after`, set through a ref with `style.setProperty` (CSSOM, allowed by `style-src 'self'`), so the DOM holds only real rows. `table-layout: fixed` and `nowrap` cells keep every row at the token height and the columns from jumping as the window moves.
Measured DOM rows (`tbody tr` without the sentinel, Chromium at 1440 x 950): 35 mid-scroll (about 27 visible rows plus 4 overscan on each side) and 29 at the end, where there is no trailing overscan; `tests/e2e/ledger.spec.ts` pins 35 and 29 with a tolerance of 3 and records the measured counts as a test annotation and a `dom-rows` console line. Total height is 100,000 x 36 px = 3.6 million px, below the maximum element height of Chromium (about 33.5 million px) and Firefox (about 17.9 million px); a result above roughly 490,000 rows would need scaled scrolling.
Below 1280 px the ledger sits in a `.scroll` region (`overflow-x: auto`, the table at a 60rem minimum width, focusable and labelled "Ledger, scrolls sideways" only while it overflows; the table caption stays "Ledger"), so narrow viewports scroll the table sideways instead of crushing columns. The cost: `overflow-x: auto` makes the region a scroll container in both axes, so the sticky `thead` sticks to that region, which never scrolls vertically, and the header scrolls away with the page below 1280 px.
Keyboard: roving focus is keyed by row key with its last index, so it survives recycling and re-sorting; Up and Down move one row, Home and End jump to the ends of the whole result, Page Up and Page Down move by the visible row count read from the live layout at key time (the window in state lags a programmatic scroll until its scroll event lands, which once paged 10 rows short) and step by the same count, the rows that touch the band from `max(stickyHeight, tbody top)` to the viewport bottom, minus one (`pageStep`), so Page Up undoes a Page Down. Each move focuses the row with `preventScroll` and then calls `scrollIntoView({ block: 'nearest' })`, whose `scroll-margin-block-start` of one row keeps it clear of the sticky header (0 below 1280 px, where the header does not stick). When a focused row is about to leave the window, focus moves first to a zero-size sentinel row inside the `tbody`, pinned to the viewport with `position: fixed` so a browser focus such as Shift+Tab never scrolls the page to it, that takes the row's `aria-rowindex`, `tabindex="0"` and a visually hidden "scrolled out of view" label, so Tab order continues from the grid; an arrow key on the sentinel continues from that row, and scrolling back hands focus back to the row. Without a focused row the tab stop is the first row wholly below `stickyHeight`, at every width. The scroll listener's first, synchronous call on mount never parks focus on the sentinel.
Selection is a `Set<number>` in page state, cleared whenever the serialised filter changes; the header checkbox selects the first 1,000 ids of the result and the action bar then says so. "Categorise n transactions" calls `setCategories` once with the selection (`setCategory` delegates to it), clears the selection and raises a toast ("Categorised n transactions as Category") in the app's polite `role="status"` toast region, which is the screen reader announcement; the query hook re-posts because the edits snapshot changed. The totals and the action bar share one slot (`.resultsSummary`; not `.summary`, which the sidebar's disclosure uses): with a non-empty selection the slot shows the action bar instead of the totals, at a fixed block size per width band (`--results-summary-block-size`: one text line plus one control row from 900 px, three rows while the slot is narrower than 36rem, the 2 x 2 totals grid below 900 px; the results column tops out near 925 px, short of the 929 px one line with the limit notice needs). The bar holds two groups, the count with the limit notice and the category Select with the button, which wrap as units and inside themselves, so selecting never shifts the table.
A route-level `ErrorBoundary` (`src/router/ErrorBoundary.tsx`) wraps the routed page, renders "Something went wrong" with Reload and the message in a `<details>`, and resets when the pathname changes; when it catches, it moves focus to its `h1` ("Something went wrong", `tabIndex={-1}`), and a failed worker query reaches it through the hook's rethrow.
The build emits `assets/ledger.worker-*.js` (19,740 bytes, 6,733 bytes gzip -9), spawned from the lazy ledger chunk as `new Worker(new URL('/assets/ledger.worker-*.js', import.meta.url), { type: 'module' })`, same origin, so `worker-src 'self'` covers it.

## Budgets and checks

- Runtime dependencies: 2, asserted by `scripts/verify-bundle.mjs`.
- Initial JavaScript gzip budget: 90 KB placeholder (`BUDGET_GZIP_BYTES`), replaced by the measured number after Stage 2.
- Lighthouse: `@lhci/cli` in Stage 5; CI carries a commented placeholder step.
- CSP source of truth is `public/staticwebapp.config.json`; `vite preview` serves its `globalHeaders`, so Playwright runs against the real header.
- The CSP has no `'unsafe-inline'`; `script-src` allows `'self'` plus one sha256 hash for the theme bootstrap in `index.html`. `verify-bundle.mjs` fails unless `dist/index.html` has exactly one inline script and its hash is in the deployed CSP, and `source-bans.test.ts` checks the same against the source files.
- Caching: a global `Cache-Control: no-cache` with an `/assets/*` immutable route; that the route-level header takes precedence over the global one is to be confirmed on the live site in Stage 5.
- `verify-bundle.mjs` reads `dist/.vite/manifest.json` to measure the initial chunks, then deletes `dist/.vite` so the manifest is not deployed.

## Deviations

- 2026-10-16, router size: `src/router/` is 355 lines of TypeScript including blank lines against SPEC.md's "about 100 lines". The extra lines are the robustness the review asked for: per-entry scroll restoration that survives Forward and reload, the replace option, pathname-versus-search gating, safe param decoding, and the Link guards for download, cross-origin and non-http links. Splitting them out would hide router behaviour rather than remove it.
- 2026-10-16, process: Stage 1 runs as four sub-stages (1a domain, 1b form primitives and theme, 1c display and overlay components, 1d design page), each its own implementer spawn with its own verify and review round, because the stage exceeds one bounded spawn.
- 2026-10-16, pending share: about 1.1 % of transactions are pending (1,138) instead of SPEC.md's 3 %. Pending is limited to the last 14 days, which hold only about 1.9 % of rows on the recency trend; re-dating 3 % of rows into that window inflated the final month and biased the forecast, so a row already dated inside the window becomes pending with probability 0.6 instead.
- 2026-10-16, interest VAT: interest carries `vatRate` 0 like revenue, salaries and tax, not 19 %, because interest income is VAT exempt.
- 2026-10-16, CSP script-src gains one sha256 hash for the theme bootstrap; no unsafe-inline. Applying the stored theme from the module bundle left a light page for 1.5 s with a delayed bundle and a stored dark theme; a hashed one-statement inline script in `<head>` sets `data-theme` before first paint, and the build check pins it to exactly one script whose hash is in the CSP.
- 2026-10-16, Switch drops `aria-checked`: SPEC.md names it, but the native checked state of `<input type="checkbox" role="switch">` already carries it and ARIA in HTML discourages `aria-checked` on checkbox inputs.
- 2026-10-16, `--color-border-strong` changes from `#9DA19B` to `#838882` (light) and from `#5A5F59` to `#6A7069` (dark): the old values measured 2.53, 2.32 and 2.62 (light) and 2.71, 2.86 and 2.46 (dark) against surface, surface-sunken and surface-raised, below the 3:1 non-text minimum; the new ones measure 3.49, 3.19 and 3.62 (light) and 3.48, 3.67 and 3.16 (dark).

- 2026-10-16, `/design` heading and route title are "Design system" (the nav label stays "Design"), so the document title and the route announcer match the h1; `tests/e2e/shell.spec.ts` follows.
